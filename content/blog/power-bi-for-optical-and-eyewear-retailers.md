An optical practice or eyewear retailer is two businesses working together: a clinical exam business and an eyewear retail business, tied together by vision insurance and by the patient relationship. Its economics depend on how well the exam converts to an eyewear sale, what margin that eyewear actually earns, and how reliably patients come back. Seeing all of this clearly is hard, because exam and retail data live in separate systems and insurance runs through both. Power BI's role is to join those systems into one governed view, so conversion, margin, and recall are visible together. This is how optical businesses use it.

## Exam and eyewear, split across systems

Ask an optical business how it is really performing, and answering it means joining two systems that usually do not talk: the **practice management or EHR system**, holding exams and patients, and the **retail or dispensing system**, holding eyewear sales, with **vision insurance** threaded through both. The practice sees exam numbers in one place and eyewear sales in another, and the connection between them, how well exams convert to sales, what the eyewear actually earns, which patients are due back, is not clearly in one view.

The first and most valuable thing Power BI does is build a **governed model that joins these systems**, so exams, eyewear sales, conversion, margin, and recall can be read together over time. Power BI reads from the practice management and retail systems, and the model relates them into one coherent picture. Once that model exists, the practice can see the **whole business**, exam and eyewear together, rather than two halves read separately. That join is the foundation, because the optical business is exam plus eyewear, linked by the patient, and no single system shows how the two actually connect.

## Exam-to-eyewear conversion: the core lever

The number at the heart of optical economics is **exam-to-eyewear conversion**: the share of patients who, after an exam, buy their eyewear from the practice. This matters because the **exam creates the opportunity** that the eyewear sale **realizes.** An exam that does not convert to an eyewear purchase is a sale the practice did the clinical work to create and then lost, often to an online retailer or another shop, so conversion is one of the biggest levers on revenue the practice has.

A governed model that **connects exams to eyewear sales** lets a practice see its conversion rate and, crucially, **where it loses patients** between the exam and the sale. With that visible, the practice can work to capture more of the eyewear its own exams create, addressing the points where patients walk without buying, whether that is selection, price perception, or the handoff from exam to dispensing. Seeing conversion is seeing the main place optical revenue is **won or lost**, because the exams are happening either way, and the difference between a practice that converts well and one that does not is enormous revenue on the same clinical volume. A practice that cannot see its conversion is managing blind to its single biggest revenue lever; one that can see it manages the thing that most decides its results.

## Margin and insurance: the real profitability

Beyond conversion, **frame and lens margin** and the effect of **vision insurance** decide profitability in ways hidden until the data is joined. Eyewear margin varies a lot by **frame, lens type, and add-ons**, and vision insurance affects **what the practice actually collects**, so two sales that look similar on the surface can have very different real profitability. A practice watching eyewear volume without seeing margin and the insurance effect can be busy and still not know where its eyewear income actually comes from.

A governed model that shows **margin by product and payer** lets a practice see which eyewear and which insurance arrangements are genuinely profitable. With that visible, it understands where its eyewear income **really comes from**, can see the impact of its insurance mix, and can manage toward the products and arrangements that actually pay rather than assuming volume means profit. For a business constantly balancing **retail margin against insurance reimbursement**, seeing the real margin behind the sales is how it protects profitability, because the insurance effect and the product mix can quietly erode margin that volume alone would suggest is healthy. Knowing which eyewear and which payers actually pay is the difference between managing the practice's profitability and hoping it works out.

## Patient recall: the recurring revenue you can miss

Optical has a built-in advantage that is **easy to waste**: patients return on a **cycle**, so there is recurring revenue in **recall**, bringing existing patients back for their next exam and eyewear update. But without a clear view of **who is due**, recall opportunities slip, and the practice leaves reliable revenue on the table, passively hoping patients remember to return rather than actively bringing them back.

A governed model that **surfaces patient recall**, identifying who is due for an exam or an eyewear update, lets the practice reach them **deliberately.** This turns recall from a passive hope into an **active process**: the practice can see who is due and contact them, bringing back patients it already has rather than relying only on new ones. For a business with a naturally recurring patient base, this is one of the most **reliable** ways to sustain and grow revenue, because the patients are already the practice's, and the only thing standing between them and a return visit is often just being reminded. Seeing and acting on recall captures revenue that the practice has essentially already earned the right to, which makes it some of the highest-return activity an optical business can do, and it depends entirely on being able to see who is due, which the governed view provides.

## It fits practices of any size

A single optical practice benefits as clearly as a group, because even one location runs exam and retail data in separate systems reconciled by hand, if at all, and conversion, margin, and recall matter just as much. Bringing it into one governed view gives a single practice a clear picture of its whole business without a data team, through a partner who builds it and a managed arrangement that keeps it running. Groups gain the ability to compare locations, but the core value, seeing conversion, margin, and recall together, applies to a single practice too, because the underlying economics are the same at any size.

## What optical businesses end up with

An optical or eyewear business running on Power BI ends up with:

- Practice management and retail data joined so the whole business can be read in one view.
- Exam-to-eyewear conversion visible, so the main revenue lever can be managed.
- Frame and lens margin and the insurance effect clear, so real profitability is seen.
- Patient recall surfaced, so recurring revenue from existing patients is actively captured.

The throughline is that an optical business stops seeing its exam and eyewear halves in separate systems and starts managing the whole, conversion, margin, and recall, on one governed view. The conversion that most decides revenue becomes visible and manageable, the real margin behind the sales becomes clear, and the recurring revenue in recall gets actively captured rather than passively missed. For a business where the exam creates the opportunity, the eyewear realizes it, and the patient returns on a cycle, seeing all three clearly is what turns the separate practice and retail data into the decisions that convert more exams, protect eyewear margin, and bring patients back.
