A museum or cultural institution runs on two things that live in different systems: an **audience**, measured in attendance and membership, and a **mix of funding**, earned income plus contributed support. Its health depends on how well it draws and retains that audience and how resilient its funding mix is, and seeing all of that clearly is hard, because ticketing, membership, and finance data are separate. Power BI's role is to join those systems into one governed view, so visitation, membership health, and funding mix are visible together. This is how cultural institutions use it.

## Audience and funding, split across systems

Ask a cultural institution how it is really doing, and answering it means pulling together systems that do not talk: **ticketing and admissions**, holding attendance, **membership and donor** systems, holding members and giving, and **finance**, holding the money, plus grants and programs. Leadership sees each piece in its own system, and the whole picture, how the audience and the funding connect, how the institution is really performing, is never clearly in one view.

The first and most valuable thing Power BI does is build a **governed model that joins these systems**, so attendance, membership, earned income, and contributed funding can be read together over time. Power BI reads from the ticketing, membership, and financial systems, and the model relates them into one coherent picture. Once that model exists, leadership can see the institution as a whole, audience and funding together, rather than separate operations each read on its own. That join is the foundation, because an institution's health spans its audience and its funding, and no single system shows both.

## Membership: recurring support to protect

**Membership** is **recurring support**, both dependable revenue and engaged audience, which makes **member retention and engagement** central to financial stability. Members provide steady income and are often the institution's most engaged visitors and most likely donors, so a strong, retained membership base is a foundation under the institution, and quiet attrition erodes it in a way that is easy to miss when membership data sits in its own system.

A governed model that **surfaces membership retention** and connects it to **how members engage**, their visits, event attendance, and giving, lets an institution understand **member value** and manage retention deliberately. It can see its retention rate and trend, which members are engaged and which are drifting, and how engagement relates to renewal and giving, so it can act to keep and deepen member relationships rather than only recruiting to replace quiet losses. For an institution whose stability rests partly on a recurring membership base, seeing and protecting that base is central to its financial health, and the connection between engagement and retention matters: an engaged member renews and gives, so seeing engagement is seeing both the value of membership and the early signs of its loss.

## The funding mix: resilience seen whole

Cultural institutions typically rely on a **mix of funding**, admissions and earned revenue, membership, donations, grants, and the **balance** among these sources shapes the institution's **resilience** to any one of them weakening. A grant that ends, a dip in attendance, a soft fundraising year, each lands differently depending on how dependent the institution is on that source, and that dependence is hard to see when the sources live in separate systems and are managed in isolation.

A governed model that brings the funding sources **together** lets leadership see the **full funding picture**: how the institution's income is composed, how dependent it is on each source, and where the risks and opportunities lie. With that visible, leadership can manage the mix **deliberately**, building earned income, strengthening membership, diversifying contributed support, so the institution is not dangerously reliant on any single source. Seeing the whole funding picture is what lets leadership **steward the institution's financial resilience** rather than managing each source in isolation and discovering a concentration risk only when a source weakens. For an institution that survives on a blend of earned and contributed income, understanding that blend as a whole is central to long-term stability, because resilience is a property of the mix, and only seeing the mix reveals it.

## It fits institutions of any size

A smaller institution benefits as clearly as a large museum, because even a modest cultural organization runs attendance, membership, and funding data across separate systems reconciled by hand, usually by a small team wearing many hats. Bringing it into one governed view gives a smaller institution a clear picture of its audience and funding without a data team, through a partner who builds it and a managed arrangement that keeps it running. Larger museums have more systems and sources, but the core value, seeing visitation, membership, and funding mix together, applies at any size, and the underlying nature of the institution is the same at any size.

## What cultural institutions end up with

A museum or cultural institution running on Power BI ends up with:

- Ticketing, membership, and finance data joined so audience and funding can be read together.
- Membership retention and engagement visible, so the recurring support base is protected.
- The earned-and-contributed funding mix seen whole, so financial resilience is managed deliberately.
- A clear picture of audience and funding instead of numbers read from separate systems.

The throughline is that a cultural institution stops seeing its audience and its funding in separate systems and starts managing the whole, visitation, membership, and funding mix, on one governed view. The recurring membership base gets seen and protected, the funding mix gets managed for resilience rather than source by source, and leadership stewards the institution on a clear picture. For an organization that draws an audience and survives on a blend of earned and contributed income, seeing both together is what turns the scattered ticketing, membership, and finance data into the decisions that keep the audience engaged and the funding resilient.
