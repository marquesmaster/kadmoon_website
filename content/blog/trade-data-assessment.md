Most analytics projects fail the same way: a big scope is agreed, budget is committed, and only then does anyone discover what the data actually looks like. The surprises, messy sources, inconsistent definitions, missing fields, show up late, when they are expensive to fix. A trade data assessment inverts that. It is a short, fixed-scope engagement that surfaces the reality of your data early and ships working reports, so the larger build is planned against facts instead of assumptions.

## What it actually is

An assessment starts from the decisions, not the data. We map the calls your leaders need to make, the questions behind them, and the systems where the answers live. Then, on a small fixed scope, we land your data, model it, and deliver the first [reports](/platform) on your real numbers. It usually runs about two weeks. The point is to compress the riskiest part of any analytics effort, finding out what the data can actually support, into a short, bounded engagement with something usable at the end.

## Why it de-risks the build

The expensive failures in analytics come from committing before you know. A [big-bang project](/blog/trade-control-tower) assumes the integrations, the model, and the KPIs up front and discovers the problems during the build. An assessment finds them first, on a small scope, where they are cheap to learn. By the time you decide on the full build, the biggest unknowns, how clean the data is, what the integrations take, which definitions are contested, are known facts. That is the difference between a plan and a hope.

## You leave with working analytics

An assessment is not a research report. At the end you have working dashboards on your own data, inside your own [tenant](/blog/analytics-in-your-own-tenant), that your team can use immediately. Even if you went no further, you would have useful [landed cost](/blog/landed-cost-calculation) or [duty exposure](/blog/effective-duty-rate) analytics that did not exist before. That is deliberate: the entry point should deliver value on its own, not just qualify you for a bigger invoice.

## It produces a grounded quote

Because the assessment has touched your real systems, the scope and quote for the full build are grounded in what was actually found, not estimated blind. You see what it will take to model each metric, which integrations are straightforward and which are not, and where the effort concentrates. A quote built on a two-week look at your data is a very different thing from one built on a discovery call and an assumption. The fee for the assessment typically credits toward the subscription, so starting small costs nothing extra if you proceed.

## Start small, prove it, then run on it

The pattern that works is not a leap; it is a ladder. Start with the assessment, see real analytics on your data, and decide on the full build with evidence. Then the [platform](/platform) runs the analytics your operation needs, and grows as your trade does. The alternative, committing to a large project on a pitch, is how analytics efforts end up over budget and under-used. Starting small and proving it is slower to feel ambitious and far more likely to work.
