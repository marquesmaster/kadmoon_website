Wanting trade analytics is easy. Justifying it on a budget line, against everything else competing for the same money, is harder, and many good programs die at that step because they are pitched on the wrong thing. The usual pitch is better visibility, more insight, a single source of truth, and finance, reasonably, discounts all of it, because none of it is a number. A business case that survives contact with a CFO is not built on the promise of seeing more; it is built on specific sums already sitting in the flow that the analytics will recover. The visibility is how the recovery happens, but the case is made with the money.

## Visibility is a benefit nobody can size

The weakness of a visibility-based case is that it describes a capability, not a return, and a return nobody can size is easy to treat as zero. Better visibility competes against proposals that come with figures attached, and it loses, not because it is worthless but because it is unquantified. The fix is not to argue harder for visibility; it is to stop leading with it. Lead instead with what the visibility lets you recover, which can be stated in dollars, and let the capability be the mechanism rather than the headline. Finance does not buy insight; it buys outcomes it can put a number on.

## The recoverable sums are specific

The money trade analytics recovers is not vague. It comes from a short list of concrete sources, each of which an importer is usually losing right now. Overpaid [duty](/blog/effective-duty-rate), from misclassification, missed [preference](/blog/duty-optimization-four-levers), or [valuation](/blog/first-sale-valuation) that could be lower. Cash trapped in [excess inventory](/blog/days-of-supply-stockout-risk) and [dead stock](/blog/dead-stock-slow-moving-inventory) beyond what lead times require. [Freight billing errors](/blog/freight-cost-analytics) and surcharge creep paid without challenge. [Duty eligible for recovery](/blog/duty-drawback-recovering-duty) that was never claimed. [Chargebacks](/blog/vendor-compliance-chargebacks) taken for preventable failures. Each is a real, recurring leak, and each can be named and sized rather than gestured at.

## Most of it can be estimated before you build

The strongest move in the whole case is to estimate the recovery before committing to the build, from a sample of the existing data. You do not need the finished platform to look at a sample of entries and find duty overpaid, to spot SKUs carrying far more cover than their lead time warrants, to pull a batch of freight invoices and find surcharge creep, or to identify recoveries that were eligible and missed. A [data assessment](/blog/trade-data-assessment) sized from real data turns the case from a promise into an estimate with evidence behind it. That pre-build number is often the most persuasive part of the pitch, because it shows the money is already there to be found, not merely hoped for.

## Pay for it from a few sources, bank the rest

A disciplined case does not try to count every possible benefit, which strains credibility. It identifies a few concrete sources whose recovery alone covers the program, and presents everything else as upside. If overpaid duty and trapped inventory between them more than pay for the build, the case is made, and the freight errors, missed recoveries, and chargebacks become additional return rather than load-bearing assumptions. This framing is both more honest and more robust: it does not depend on soft benefits materializing, and it leaves room to exceed the promise rather than scramble to meet it.

## Recovery, not cost

Framed this way, trade analytics stops being a cost to be justified and becomes a recovery to be captured. A program pitched as a cost must argue its benefits beat its price, inviting a hunt for the soft benefits to discount. A program pitched as recovery points at money the operation is already bleeding and positions the analytics as the way to stop the bleed and get it back. That matches how finance thinks, and it changes the question from whether to spend into how quickly the recovery lands and how much of it is durable. The [engagement](/docs/engagement-models) and [pricing](/pricing) then sit against a return that was estimated before a dollar was committed.
