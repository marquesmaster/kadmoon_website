Ask most importers what duty rate they pay and they will quote a schedule number. That number is almost never what actually leaves their account. The base duty is only the first layer; Section 301 and 232 tariffs, antidumping and countervailing duties, and federal fees stack on top. The figure that captures all of it is the effective duty rate, and it is the single most useful duty number an import operation can track.

## What it is

Effective duty rate is total duties and fees divided by total entered value, blended across your entries. If you paid $1.2 million in duties and fees on $18 million of entered value, your effective rate is about 6.7%, whatever the schedule says for any one product. It is a weighted average of everything that hit your imports, which is exactly why it reflects reality in a way a single schedule rate cannot.

## Why it differs from the headline rate

The [tariff schedule](/docs/glossary) rate is the base duty for one classification. On top of it can sit [Section 301 and 232 tariffs](/blog/tariff-exposure-section-232-301), antidumping and countervailing duties, and the merchandise processing and harbor maintenance fees. A product with a 2% base rate can carry a double-digit effective rate once a special tariff applies. Across a whole book of different products and origins, the blend produces an effective rate that no single line would reveal, and that blend is what shows up in cash.

## Why it is the number to manage

A headline rate is a fact about a product. The effective rate is a fact about your business: it is the share of goods value you are paying to import, and it is directly comparable over time and across suppliers, origins, and product lines. Watching it move tells you whether tariff changes are hitting you, and whether the actions you take to reduce duty are working. It is the trade equivalent of a blended cost of goods: the number a CFO can actually use.

## Broken down, it becomes actionable

The blended rate alone is a headline. The value is in the breakdown: effective rate by product line, by origin, by supplier, and the [tariff stack](/blog/tariff-exposure-section-232-301) that makes it up. That decomposition shows where duty concentrates and which lever applies, FTA [preference](/blog/usmca-2026-review-importers), a [Foreign-Trade Zone](/blog/foreign-trade-zone-duty-deferral), [drawback](/blog/duty-drawback-recovering-duty), a classification fix, or re-sourcing. Without the breakdown, the rate is a number you worry about; with it, it is a map of where to act.

## Why keeping it current is an analytics job

The effective rate is not static. Entries arrive, classifications get corrected, tariff rates change by proclamation, and the product mix shifts. Computing it once in a spreadsheet is easy; keeping it current across a live import book is not, which is why most operations only see it at quarter end if at all.
