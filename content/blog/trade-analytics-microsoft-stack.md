If your business already runs on Microsoft, there is a reassuring answer to a question trade buyers often ask early: yes, your foreign-trade analytics can run on the same stack, in the same tenant, without moving your data anywhere. Kadmoon delivers landed cost, duty exposure, shipment visibility, and compliance analytics on Microsoft Fabric, Power BI, and Azure, inside your own Microsoft environment. The Microsoft stack is the delivery; the foreign-trade model built on top of it is the offer.

## The stack, and what each part does

The three Microsoft technologies play distinct roles. [Microsoft Fabric](/platform) is the unified data foundation, where the data from your systems is landed and the governed trade model is built. Power BI is the reporting layer, where the dashboards and alerts your team opens every morning actually live. Azure is the cloud underneath it all, inside your tenant. None of this is exotic for a company already on Microsoft; it is the stack you likely already pay for, used for the one job it was not set up to do on its own, which is foreign-trade analytics.

## It runs in your own tenant

The reason to deliver on the Microsoft stack, rather than in a vendor platform, is ownership. The analytics runs in [your own tenant](/blog/analytics-in-your-own-tenant), so your trade data never leaves your environment, stays under your own security and access controls, and is not pooled somewhere you cannot see. Your team signs in to Power BI in your tenant, not to a Kadmoon login. If you do not run Microsoft yet, we can provision and manage a dedicated tenant for you, but even then the data is isolated and the model, reports, and data are yours, documented and handed over.

## The tools are not the value

It is worth being clear about what Microsoft gives you and what it does not. Fabric and Power BI are powerful, but a BI tool is an empty canvas: it does not know what [landed cost](/blog/landed-cost-calculation) or [duty exposure](/blog/effective-duty-rate) mean, how freight should be allocated to the unit, or what counts as on time for a cross-border shipment. The value Kadmoon adds is the [governed trade data model](/blog/semantic-layer-trade-kpi) built on the stack: the KPI definitions, the connectors from your ERP, broker, carrier, [EDI and ACE](/blog/edi-ace-integration-for-trade), and spreadsheets, and the reports that turn all of it into decisions specific to foreign trade. The stack runs it; the model makes it mean something.

## If you already run Power BI

Many trade operations already have Power BI and some reports in it. In that case Kadmoon does not replace anything; it adds the trade model underneath. Your existing Power BI stays, and what changes is that it is now fed by a governed model built for trade rather than assembled report by report, so landed cost means one thing across every report and each number traces back to the shipment or entry behind it. This is the difference between having the tool and having the [trade intelligence platform](/blog/what-is-a-trade-intelligence-platform) the tool was missing.

## Reusing what you already pay for

For a company already invested in Microsoft, delivering trade analytics on the same stack is efficient as well as familiar. There is no second platform to buy, learn, and govern alongside the one you already run; the analytics reuses your Fabric, Power BI, and Azure, and the Microsoft capacity can even be billed on one invoice with the platform if you want us to manage it. The result is foreign-trade analytics that fits the environment you already have, owned by you, rather than another system bolted on beside it. That is the whole idea behind the [platform](/platform): one trade intelligence layer, on the Microsoft stack, in your tenant.
