There is a question worth asking any Power BI consultant before a build starts, and it is not about dashboards or price. It is: whose environment will this be built in? The answer decides something more important than how the reports look. It decides whether, at the end, you own your reporting or merely have access to it. Kadmoon builds everything in the client's own Microsoft tenant, and the reasoning is worth spelling out, because the alternative is common and the difference is easy to miss until it costs you.

## Two ways a build can be delivered

In the first, the consultant builds in your own Microsoft tenant. The model, the reports, the data, and the security all live in your environment. When the build is done, it is handed to you with documentation and training, and it stays exactly where it was built, in your hands.

In the second, the consultant builds in their own environment and gives you access to the result. You can open the reports and see your numbers, but the thing producing them lives on their side. You are, in effect, renting access to your own reporting. As long as the relationship continues and the access holds, this can look identical to the first arrangement. The difference shows up the moment anything changes.

## Ownership is about what happens next

The value of owning the build in your tenant is not visible on day one. It is visible when you want to extend the reports, bring in another team, move a person off the work, or part ways with the provider. If the build is in your tenant, all of that is straightforward: the reporting is yours, it stays with you, and anyone you choose can pick it up from the documentation.

If the build is in the consultant's environment, each of those moments is harder. Extending it depends on them. Changing providers may mean migrating the work out, if that is offered at all, and some of it may not transfer cleanly. Your reporting has become a dependency on one relationship, and the cost of ending that relationship is losing or rebuilding what you paid for. Ownership removes that trap. It makes continuing with a provider a choice you make because the work is good, not because leaving is painful.

## Where your data sits

Whose tenant the build lives in also decides where your data sits. In your own tenant, your data stays within your organization's Microsoft environment, under your security, your access controls, and your governance. Row-level security, access management, and compliance all operate inside the boundary you already control and already audit. There is no copy of your data sitting somewhere you cannot see.

Built in a consultant's environment, your data may be pooled alongside other clients' in an arrangement you do not set the rules for and have to take on trust. For anything sensitive, which most financial and operational data is, that is a governance question you should not have to accept as a side effect of how the reporting was built. Keeping the data in your tenant keeps the security model yours.

## Why we build this way on principle

Kadmoon builds in the client's own tenant as a matter of principle, not as an upsell. We never pool client data in our own environment. The model, the reports, and the data are built and kept in your Microsoft tenant, documented and handed over, so what you receive is an asset you hold rather than a service you depend on us to keep alive.

This shapes how we work. Because you own the result, our incentive is to make the work good enough that you want to keep working with us, not to make leaving difficult. A build you own in your own tenant is yours to maintain, extend, or move on from. That is the arrangement a buyer should expect, and it is the one we deliver by default.

## The question to ask up front

Before any Power BI build, ask where it will be built and who will own it at the end. If the answer is your own tenant, with the model, reports, and data handed to you, you are buying an asset. If the answer is the consultant's environment with access granted to you, you are renting one, and the terms of that rental will matter most at the moment you most want flexibility. The tenant question is small to ask and expensive to overlook, which is why it belongs at the start of the conversation, not the end.
