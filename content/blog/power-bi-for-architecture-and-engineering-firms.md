An architecture or engineering firm makes its money one project at a time, and manages its future by how its people's time is spent and how much work is committed ahead. Running it well means seeing project margin, utilization, and backlog clearly and in time, and that is hard, because the data lives in project accounting, time capture, and resourcing systems that do not reconcile, and the numbers that matter most often surface only when a project closes, too late to change anything. Power BI's role for an AEC firm is to join those systems into one governed view, so margin, utilization, and backlog are visible while there is still time to act. This is how architecture and engineering firms use it.

## The numbers live in separate systems

Ask an AEC firm a question that should be central, which of our projects are actually making money, who is over or under utilized, and how much work do we have committed ahead, and the answer usually takes someone pulling from project accounting, exporting time, checking the resourcing schedule, and assembling it by hand. Project accounting holds fees and costs, the time system holds hours, resourcing holds who is on what, and none of them reconciles with the others. The real picture of a project's health and the firm's capacity is reconstructed manually, and by the time it is assembled it is already out of date.

The first and most valuable thing Power BI does is build a **governed model that joins these systems**, so project margin, utilization, and backlog can be read together by project, team, discipline, and over time. Power BI reads from project accounting, time and expense, and resourcing, and the model relates them into one coherent picture. Once that model exists, the questions that took manual assembly become reports the firm opens, current rather than stale. That join is the foundation, because a firm's health spans these systems, and no single one holds the whole picture.

## Margin seen in time, not at close

The reason this matters most for project margin is timing. **Project margin discovered at close** is a historical fact: useful for learning, but the project is over and nothing can be done about it. **Margin seen while the project runs** is a live signal the firm can still act on. The governed model makes the difference, by tracking fees against committed and incurred cost and hours burned against the plan, as the project goes, so the firm sees a project's margin developing rather than learning it after the fact.

This changes what a firm can do. A project trending toward thin or negative margin shows up while there is still runway to respond, by managing scope, adjusting staffing, addressing a change in the work with the client, or catching a cost overrun before it compounds. The firm protects margin it would otherwise only have recorded as a loss at close. For a business whose profit is made or lost project by project, this is the core of what the analytics provides: margin as something to **manage** in time, not a number to **explain** afterward. A firm that sees its project margins developing manages its profitability; a firm that sees them only at close manages its explanations.

## Utilization and backlog: busy, profitably, and ahead

Beyond individual projects, a firm's health rests on two more numbers the governed view brings together. **Utilization** shows whether people's time is going to billable project work, which is the engine of a services firm's economics, and where it is low, where effort is leaking to non-billable work, or where people are stretched too thin to do good work. **Backlog** shows how much committed work lies ahead, which tells the firm whether its pipeline is healthy, thin, or more than current capacity can absorb.

Read together in a governed model, utilization and backlog answer a question no single number does: is the firm busy, **profitably** busy, and busy **for long enough ahead?** High utilization with thin backlog is a warning that the work runs out soon. Healthy backlog with low utilization points to a resourcing or delivery problem. Seeing both together lets the firm manage capacity and pipeline deliberately, pursuing work when backlog thins, staffing up when it is deep, and keeping utilization in the band where people are productive without being overloaded. This is how a firm avoids the familiar lurch between frantic and idle, by managing to a clear view of workload and what lies ahead rather than reacting to whichever one is currently hurting.

## It fits firms of any size

A smaller AEC firm benefits as clearly as a large one, because even a small practice runs projects whose margin, time, and resourcing data sit in separate systems and get reconciled by hand, often by a principal whose time is better spent on the work itself. Bringing it into one governed view gives a smaller firm a clear picture of margin, utilization, and backlog without a data team, through a partner who builds it and a managed arrangement that keeps it running. Larger firms run more projects and more people, but the core value, margin in time and capacity clearly, applies at any size, and smaller firms often feel the manual pain most because the reporting falls on the people least able to spare the hours.

## What AEC firms end up with

An architecture or engineering firm running on Power BI ends up with:

- Project accounting, time, and resourcing joined so projects and capacity can be read whole.
- Project margin visible while projects run, so it can be protected rather than recorded at close.
- Utilization and backlog together, so the firm sees whether it is busy, profitably, and ahead.
- A clear current view without the manual system-by-system assembly or a data team.

The throughline is that an AEC firm stops reconstructing its project health by hand from systems that disagree, and stops learning its margins only when projects close, and starts managing projects and capacity on one governed, current view. Margin develops in sight and gets protected in time, utilization and backlog show whether the workload is right and continues, and the firm runs its projects and its pipeline on evidence. For a business that earns its living project by project and lives on how its people's time is spent, seeing margin in time and capacity clearly is what turns the project and time data the firm already captures into the decisions that protect its profit and keep its people productively at work.
