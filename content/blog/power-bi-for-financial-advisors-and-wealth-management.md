A financial advisory or wealth management firm is, in the end, its book: the assets it manages and the client relationships behind them. Managing and growing that book means seeing it clearly, whose assets, which relationships, what is growing, what is at risk, and that is harder than it should be, because the data lives in portfolio, custodial, CRM, and planning systems that do not reconcile, and it is sensitive enough that the reporting must be tightly controlled. Power BI's role for an advisory firm is to join those systems into one governed view, so the book is visible and the client data stays protected. This is how advisory firms use it.

## The book is split across systems

Ask an advisory firm a question that should be central, how is our book growing, which relationships are strong or at risk, and how are our advisors performing, and the answer usually takes someone pulling reports from the portfolio system and assembling them by hand. Portfolio and custodial data are in one place, the CRM holds the relationships, planning tools hold another view. Each holds a piece, none reconciles with the others, and the real picture of the book is reconstructed manually, late.

The first and most valuable thing Power BI does is build a **governed model that joins these systems**, so AUM, client relationships, and practice metrics can be read together by advisor, client segment, and over time. Power BI reads from the portfolio, custodial, CRM, and planning systems, and the model relates them into one coherent picture of the book. Once that model exists, the questions that took manual assembly become reports the firm opens, current rather than stale. That join is the foundation, because the book spans the systems, and no single one holds the whole picture.

## Seeing the book, growing the book

The reason this matters is that a firm manages and grows its book by **seeing it clearly**, and the governed view reveals where growth and risk actually are. Seeing **AUM and its movement** by advisor, client, and segment shows where the book is growing and where it is shrinking. Seeing **client relationships and their health** surfaces which are strong, which are at risk of leaving, and where there is opportunity to deepen them. Seeing **advisor and segment performance** shows what is driving results.

A governed model brings this together so the firm manages its book on **evidence** rather than impression: directing attention to at-risk relationships before they leave, pursuing growth opportunities where they are, and understanding which segments and advisors drive results so the firm can build on them. For a firm whose entire value is its book, seeing the book clearly is directly seeing how to protect and grow it, which is the core of what the analytics provides. A firm that can see where its AUM is moving and where its relationships stand manages its book deliberately; a firm reconstructing that picture by hand manages by feel, which in a business this is costly.

## Security for sensitive client data

Client financial data is among the most sensitive a firm holds, and the sector is regulated, so the security story is central. Built in the firm's **own controlled Microsoft environment**, client data stays under the firm's control, with access restricted appropriately and activity logged, rather than moving to an outside platform.

**Row-level security** in the model restricts who sees which clients and books, so an advisor sees their own book and leadership sees the firm, all from the same governed reports, with the rules defined once and inherited everywhere. Access follows the firm's identity provider with multi-factor authentication. For sensitive, regulated client financial data, having access defined once in the model and provable through logs is what makes it both controlled and auditable, which is exactly what the sector requires and what doing this responsibly demands. The reporting is useful and the client data is protected, which for an advisory firm is not a trade-off to balance but a requirement to meet, and the governed model in the firm's own environment is how both are met at once.

## It fits firms of any size

A smaller advisory firm benefits as clearly as a large one, because even a solo or small-team practice runs on portfolio, client, and planning data scattered across systems and pulled by hand. Bringing it into one governed view gives a smaller firm a clear picture of its book and clients without the manual work and without a data team, through a partner who builds it and a managed arrangement that keeps it running. Larger firms manage more advisors and more data, but the core value, one clear view of the book and the clients, applies at any size, because the underlying problem, scattered data reconciled by hand, is the same whether the firm has one advisor or many.

## What advisory firms end up with

An advisory firm running on Power BI ends up with:

- Portfolio, custodial, CRM, and planning data joined so the book can be read whole.
- AUM, relationships, and advisor performance visible, so growth and risk are clear and actionable.
- Client financial data kept in the firm's controlled environment, with row-level security and logging.
- A clear current view of the book without the manual portfolio-system reports or a data team.

The throughline is that an advisory firm stops reconstructing its book by hand from systems that disagree and starts managing and growing it on one governed, current view where AUM, relationships, and performance are clear, with the sensitive client data controlled. The at-risk relationships surface in time, the growth opportunities become visible, and the firm manages its book on evidence. For a business whose value is its book of assets and relationships, seeing that book clearly and currently, with the client data protected, is what turns the portfolio and client data the firm already holds into the deliberate management and growth of the thing the firm is ultimately worth.
