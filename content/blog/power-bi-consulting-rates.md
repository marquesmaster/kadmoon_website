Ask five firms what Power BI consulting costs and you will get five different rate cards, which is exactly why the rate is the wrong thing to compare. What actually determines your bill is the pricing *model* and who does the work. This guide breaks down the three ways Power BI consulting is sold, what each really costs you in risk as well as dollars, and how to compare offers on the number that matters: total cost of the outcome.

## The three pricing models

- **Hourly.** You pay for time. Rates swing widely with seniority and region, from cheap junior or offshore work to premium senior specialists. The problem is not the rate; it is that hourly billing puts the risk of drift on you and quietly rewards a project that takes longer.
- **Fixed-scope projects.** You pay an agreed price for a defined outcome, quoted after a short assessment. The risk of estimation sits with the provider, and you know the cost and timeline before delivery starts.
- **Capacity retainers.** For ongoing work, a fixed monthly fee buys a [defined pool of hours plus an SLA](/packages), and you request work freely within those hours. Predictable for you, sustainable for the provider.

## Why the rate is the wrong number

A low hourly rate feels like a saving and often is not. Cheap rates usually mean junior delivery, which means more hours, more rework, and a model you may have to rebuild later. A senior, [certified consultant](/services/power-bi) at a higher rate scopes tightly, reuses proven patterns, and gets to a trustworthy result in fewer hours. Two engagements can quote very different rates and land at a similar total, or the "expensive" one can come in cheaper. Always compare the total cost of the outcome and who is doing the work, not the sticker rate.

## When fixed price wins

For any build with a recognizable shape, a report set, a semantic model, a [migration](/tableau-to-power-bi-migration), fixed price is the safer buy. The provider absorbs the estimation risk, you get a number and a date before anyone starts, and scope changes are explicit rather than absorbed into a growing invoice. The precondition is a short assessment first, so the fixed price is based on your actual data instead of a guess. Be wary of a fixed price quoted before anyone has looked at your estate; that is not confidence, it is a number that will be renegotiated.

## When a retainer wins

Once Power BI is in production, the work is continuous, not a project: refreshes to watch, governance to maintain, new reports as the business changes. That is a poor fit for both hourly (every request is a negotiation) and one-off projects (they end). A [capacity retainer](/packages) fits it exactly: a fixed monthly fee for a pool of hours and an SLA, priced by how much capacity and how fast a response you need. The one shape to avoid is genuinely "unlimited," which looks generous but pressures the provider to slow delivery once you lean on it.

## How to compare offers

Put the rate aside and ask four things: who actually does the delivery, how scope changes are priced, what the total cost of the defined outcome is, and where the work lives and who owns it. The offer that answers those cleanly, senior delivery, explicit change handling, a fixed number for the outcome, and your tenant, is almost always the better buy, whatever the hourly figure says.
