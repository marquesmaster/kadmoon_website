The phrase trade intelligence platform gets used loosely enough that it is worth saying plainly what one is, because the looseness hides a real distinction. A trade intelligence platform is not a dashboard, not a BI tool, and not another application to adopt. It is four things delivered as one: a governed data model of your trade, a library of trade KPIs defined once, the connectors that feed the model from your source systems, and the reports and alerts built on top. Kadmoon Trade Intelligence is our name for that combination. Understanding why those four belong together, and what the platform is not, is the difference between buying analytics that works and buying a tool that leaves you to build it yourself.

## The four things, and why they are one

The four parts are not a menu; they depend on each other. The [connectors](/docs/data-integration) pull from your ERP, broker, carrier, EDI and ACE, and spreadsheets. They feed a [governed model](/blog/semantic-layer-trade-kpi) where each KPI, landed cost, duty exposure, OTIF, is defined a single time. The [KPI library](/docs/kpi-library) is that set of definitions, built for foreign trade rather than invented per report. And the reports and alerts are what your team actually opens, each number drillable to the shipment or entry behind it. Separate the four and you have pieces that do not add up: connectors with no model, a model with no reports, reports with no governance. Together they are a platform; apart they are a project that never quite finishes.

## It is not a BI tool

The most common confusion is with a BI tool like Power BI, and the distinction matters. A BI tool draws charts. It is an empty canvas with no opinion about what landed cost means, how duty exposure is calculated, or what counts as on time for a cross-border shipment. Hand a trade operation a BI license and you have handed it the tools to build a platform, not a platform. The trade intelligence platform arrives with the model, the KPIs, and the connectors already built, so you begin from a governed model of your trade rather than a blank screen. It runs on Power BI; it is not replaced by it. The tool is how the reports are drawn; the platform is what makes them mean something.

## It is not an app you adopt

The second confusion is with software you deploy. A trade intelligence platform is delivered on the Microsoft stack, Fabric, Power BI, and Azure, [in your own tenant](/blog/analytics-in-your-own-tenant), which means there is nothing to install and no separate application to log into. Your team signs in to Power BI, where the analytics already runs, not to a Kadmoon product. What you are buying is the governed model, the KPIs, the connectors, and the maintained reports, kept current as a subscription. Framing it as an app to adopt misses the point: the platform meets your data where it lives and runs in your environment, rather than asking you to move your operation into someone else's.

## The value is the model underneath

Strip away the reports and the tool, and what makes a trade intelligence platform valuable is the governed trade model underneath. That model is what makes a number in one report line up with the same number in another, what makes [landed cost](/blog/landed-cost-calculation) mean one thing across the whole operation, and what makes the analytics specific to foreign trade rather than generic. It is also what makes the numbers trustworthy under challenge, because each one carries its [lineage](/blog/data-lineage-trade-analytics) back to the source. The reports are what people see, but the model is what they are trusting, and it is the part a generic tool cannot give you because it has no knowledge of trade to build in.

## You own it

The last defining feature is ownership. Because the platform runs in your tenant, the model, the reports, and the data are yours, documented and handed over. The subscription keeps them current, connectors maintained, KPIs governed, reports evolving as your trade changes, but it does not hold the asset hostage. If you ever stop, you keep operating on what you own. That is the opposite of a platform you rent access to and lose the moment you leave. It is also why the engagement [starts small](/blog/first-90-days-trade-analytics), with an assessment and first reports, and grows into a running subscription: you are building an asset in your own environment, one that climbs the [maturity ladder](/blog/trade-analytics-maturity-model) as your decisions demand more of it.

If you have been offered a trade intelligence platform and were not sure what you would actually be getting, the answer is a governed model, the KPIs, the connectors, and the reports, running in your tenant and owned by you.
