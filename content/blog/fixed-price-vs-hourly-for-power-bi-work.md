There are two common ways to price Power BI work, and the difference between them is mostly about who carries the risk. Hourly billing puts the risk of a vague scope on the client, because every hour of uncertainty is billed to them. Fixed scope and fixed price put that risk on the people doing the work. Kadmoon prices project work fixed, and this is the reasoning behind that choice, what it requires to be honest, and where a monthly plan fits instead.

## What hourly billing really charges for

An hourly rate sounds transparent: you pay for the time the work takes. The problem is that it also charges you for time spent discovering what the work was in the first place. If the scope was never pinned down, the hours spent figuring out the real sources, the right measures, and the actual deliverables are all billed to you. The less clear the scope, the more of those hours there are, and all of them are your cost.

This is not usually bad faith. It is where the incentive sits. When every hour is paid regardless, there is little pressure to scope carefully before starting, because the uncertainty is the client's expense, not the provider's. The result for you is that you cannot know the total in advance, and the vaguer the project, the more that total tends to grow.

## What fixed scope changes

Fixed scope and fixed price reverse who carries the risk. When the price is fixed, overruns are the provider's cost, not yours, so the provider has every reason to define the scope precisely and work efficiently. You know the deliverables and the price before the work starts, and you are not watching a meter.

But this only works honestly if the scope is actually defined first. A fixed price set before the scope is clear is a guess, and guesses go one of two bad ways: padded high to cover the unknown, or set low and then clawed back through change requests. Neither is better than hourly. For fixed pricing to be real, the definition has to come before the number.

## Why the assessment comes first

This is the reason a paid assessment starts the engagement. Before a fixed price can be set, someone has to connect to the real data, confirm the sources and measures, and agree the deliverables. The assessment does that. It is a short first project, not free pre-sales work, and it produces two things: working dashboards on your real data, and the plan and fixed quote for the full build.

After the assessment, the fixed price is grounded in what the work actually involves, so it can be held to. The scope is written down, the deliverables are agreed, and the price reflects them. That is what makes fixed pricing a fair arrangement rather than a gamble in one direction or the other. Kadmoon prices the assessment at $5,000 and a dashboard build as fixed scope from $20,000, with the build's exact scope set by the assessment.

## Where a monthly plan fits instead

Fixed project pricing suits work with a clear end: build this model, deliver these reports, hand them over. Once that is done, the need usually shifts to something without a fixed deliverable, steady changes, new measures, report tweaks, refresh monitoring, small additions as the business evolves. There is no single thing to price there, because the work is a flow rather than a project.

For that, a monthly plan fits better than either a fixed project price or an hourly meter. Kadmoon's Managed Power BI is that plan: a monthly block of work for keeping an estate healthy and evolving it as needs change, from $3,500 a month. It is not a third pricing trick; it is the right shape for ongoing work, just as fixed pricing is the right shape for a defined project.

## Choosing between them

The choice is not really fixed versus hourly. It is project versus flow. If the work ahead is a defined deliverable, fixed scope and fixed price give you known cost and known outcomes, with the risk of a vague scope carried by the people best placed to manage it. If the work ahead is ongoing upkeep and evolution, a monthly plan matches it better. Hourly billing, by contrast, mostly serves the provider, by charging you for the uncertainty that careful scoping would have removed. Pricing work fixed, after a real assessment, is how we keep that uncertainty off you.
