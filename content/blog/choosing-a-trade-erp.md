Most importers and exporters do not start with a trade ERP. They start with a generic one, or with accounting software and spreadsheets, and they make it work until the seams show: landed cost that is always a month behind, compliance done by hand, and a different system for every part of the flow. A trade ERP is what replaces that patchwork with software that understands cross-border trade in the first place. Here is how to evaluate one.

## What makes an ERP a trade ERP

The difference is the model underneath. A generic ERP assumes you buy something and sell it, with cost equal to the purchase price. Trade breaks that assumption. The real cost of a product includes freight, duty, insurance, and fees, and the operation runs on shipments, documents, incoterms, currencies, and compliance. A [trade ERP](/services/trade-erp) treats all of that as native: purchase orders and shipments built for import, [landed cost](/blog/landed-cost-calculation) allocated to the unit, multi-currency throughout, and [customs and compliance](/services/customs-compliance) in the flow rather than off to the side.

## The capabilities that matter

When comparing options, look for the parts a generic ERP forces into spreadsheets:

- Purchase-order, shipment, and document management designed around trade, not retrofitted.
- Landed cost allocated automatically as freight, duty, and fees arrive.
- Multi-currency pricing, costing, and reporting.
- Customs classification and screening connected to the order flow.
- Integration with carriers, brokers, marketplaces, and ACE, so data moves instead of being rekeyed.

If a product treats these as add-ons rather than core, you are buying a generic ERP with a trade sticker on it.

## The failure to avoid

The most common and expensive mistake is extending a generic ERP with enough workarounds to limp through trade. It looks cheaper because you already own the ERP, but the workarounds become the operation's weak point. Landed cost lives in a spreadsheet and is always stale. Compliance is a manual checklist. Each new requirement is another bolt-on. Over time the maintenance cost of the workarounds exceeds what trade-native software would have cost.

## Buy, tailor, or build

Not every operation needs custom software, and not every operation fits a product. Adopt a ready platform when your trade looks like most trade, since there is no value in rebuilding what already works. [Tailor or build](/services/custom-development) when a genuine difference in how you source, move, or sell would be forced into a template and resisted every day. Many operations land on a mix: a proven platform for the common parts, custom work where they are actually different.

## Ownership and integration

Two questions decide whether a trade ERP is a tool or a trap. First, do you own the software and the data, deployed where you control it, with documentation and a handover? Software that holds your operation hostage is not worth the fit. Second, does it integrate with what you already use, your carriers, brokers, marketplaces, and CBP? A trade ERP that cannot exchange data turns your team into a data-entry function and defeats the purpose.
