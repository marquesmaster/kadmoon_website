Duty is charged on customs value, and for most importers customs value is simply the price they paid for the goods. That is clean when you buy straight from the factory. It gets more interesting when the goods pass through a middleman first, because then the price you paid includes the middleman's markup, and under the usual approach you pay duty on that markup too. First sale valuation is the lever that, in the right circumstances, lets you pay duty on the earlier price instead. It is legitimate, long-established, and narrow, and whether it is worth pursuing is a question your data can answer.

## Duty rides on the price you paid

The default basis for customs value is transaction value, the price actually paid for the goods. When you import directly, that is the manufacturer's price and there is nothing between you and the factory. But many goods reach a US importer through an intermediary, a trading company or a related distributor, who bought from the manufacturer and sold on to you at a higher price. Pay duty on that higher price and you are paying duty on the intermediary's margin as well as on the goods, which for dutiable products at meaningful [duty rates](/blog/effective-duty-rate) is real money spent on a markup rather than on the product itself.

## Reaching back to the earlier sale

First sale addresses exactly that situation. In qualifying multi-tier transactions, it permits the importer to declare the earlier sale in the chain, typically the manufacturer-to-middleman price, as the customs value, rather than the later price the importer paid. Because [customs value](/blog/customs-value-valuation) is the base duty is calculated on, declaring the lower earlier price lowers the duty, without changing the goods, the supplier, or the commercial arrangement itself. It is one of the valuation moves that sits under the [accuracy lever](/blog/duty-optimization-four-levers) of duty optimization, and it is among the more powerful ones when the structure supports it.

## It is narrow and demanding

First sale is not a lever every importer can pull, and it is not one to pull casually. It applies only where goods genuinely move through one or more intermediaries, where the earlier transaction truly was a sale for export to the United States, and where it meets the legal conditions around being arm's length. Critically, it demands documentation that proves the earlier price and the destination of the goods, held to a standard that will withstand scrutiny. If you buy directly from the manufacturer, there is no earlier price and no lever. If the structure exists but the documentation does not, the lever is unsafe to use. The requirements are the point, not an afterthought.

## The worth of it is measurable

The question of whether first sale is worth the effort is not a matter of opinion; it is a calculation. The saving is the duty difference between the price you paid and the qualifying earlier price, summed across the goods that move through a qualifying structure. If little of your flow passes through middlemen, or the markups are thin, or the duty rates are low, the saving may not justify the documentation burden. If a large share of your [import value](/blog/country-of-origin-analytics) moves through intermediaries on dutiable goods with real markups, the saving can be substantial. That figure, duty on the markup across qualifying flow, is exactly what import data can produce before any structure is examined by counsel.

## Size it before you pursue it

The sensible order is to size the opportunity first and engage the legal and documentation work second, on the flow where the numbers justify it. Analytics can identify which goods move through intermediaries, estimate the markup embedded in the paid price, and quantify the duty riding on it, turning first sale from a vague possibility into a ranked list of where the saving actually lives. That keeps the demanding documentation effort focused on the transactions that repay it, rather than spread across flow that was never going to qualify or never going to save much. Pursuing first sale is ultimately a legal exercise, but deciding whether to pursue it is a [data](/platform) one.
